Speed Up Synology NAS Cloud Mounts (Full Guide)

Slow transfers or stuttering playback after mounting a cloud drive? Optimize in order across the drive, the network and caching to make cloud files on your NAS run faster.

1. Locate the Bottleneck: Three Speed Factors

"Cloud drive mounts are slow" is a vague statement. In practice the bottleneck can only come from the three places below, so separate them first to treat the right one:

  • Drive-side throttling: the download speed limit a drive imposes on non-members, the most common ceiling
  • Local network bandwidth: whether the cable, switch and router reach gigabit or 2.5G, and whether the cable spec is adequate
  • NAS processing and caching: whether disk read/write, memory and cache settings become the bottleneck, especially with many small files
Quick diagnosis: Transfer a file over the local NAS network to measure, then download the same file type directly through the drive client to compare. If local is fast but the drive is slow, the bottleneck is drive-side; if local is already slow, the problem is the network or the NAS.

2. Network-Layer Optimization

The network is where most users see the fastest results, with low change cost and direct benefit:

  • Cable and switch: use Cat6 or higher cabling and make sure the NAS, switch and router all support gigabit; upgrade to 2.5G where possible to raise internal throughput
  • Disable SMB1: force SMB 3.0 or later, which is more efficient and more secure
  • Adjust MTU: setting MTU to 1492 reduces packet fragmentation and improves mapped disk transfers
  • Router QoS: enable QoS on the router to prioritize NAS data traffic

3. NAS-Layer Optimization

When the bottleneck is the NAS itself, focus on read/write and caching capability:

  • Add SSD cache: a clear gain for small-file reads and frequent access; testing has shown roughly 3 times faster small-file read/write
  • Set a sensible cache limit: a larger mount cache gives a higher hit rate but also uses local space, so balance it against available memory
  • Reduce concurrent mounts: mounting too many drives at once worsens resource contention; do not keep them all resident unless necessary
  • Defragment and check disks regularly: defragment mechanical disks periodically and keep them healthy so the disk itself does not drag on speed

4. Drive-Side Optimization

If the bottleneck really is drive-side, it comes down to choosing the right drive and using it sensibly:

  • Prefer drives without speed limits: Aliyun Drive and Quark Drive perform better under no-throttle policies
  • Subscribe when it matters: for speed-sensitive drives, membership clearly raises the speed ceiling
  • Avoid peak hours: transfer speeds during network peaks are usually lower than in off-peak periods
  • Use cross-drive transfer: when migrating resources use cross-drive transfer instead of downloading locally and re-uploading

5. Playback Optimization: Avoid Transcoding

Stuttering playback is often not "not enough bandwidth" but the player transcoding in real time. The idea is to let the player play the original file directly:

  • Use players that support direct playback, such as Jellyfin or Video Station, to reduce transcoding overhead
  • Make sure the playback resolution and bitrate stay within the drive's real-time transfer capability, lowering the bitrate if needed
  • Use wired or stable Wi-Fi at the playback end to avoid stutter caused by weak wireless signals

7. FAQ

Q: Slow transfers after mounting a cloud drive on Synology - is it the NAS?

A: Not necessarily. Speed limits usually come from three places: drive-side throttling, local network bandwidth, and NAS processing and caching. Test a local network transfer and a direct drive download separately to compare, so you can tell which layer is the bottleneck instead of upgrading hardware blindly.

Q: Does adding SSD cache speed up cloud drive mounting?

A: It helps noticeably for small-file and frequently accessed workloads; testing has shown SSD cache can raise small-file read/write speed by roughly 3 times. For large sequential transfers or scenarios limited by total drive bandwidth, the gain is limited, so judge against the actual bottleneck.

Q: Do disabling SMB1 and tuning MTU really speed things up?

A: They are effective for local network transfers. Disabling SMB1 forces SMB3 or later, which is more efficient and secure, and setting MTU to 1492 reduces packet fragmentation. Combined with Cat6 cabling and a gigabit or 2.5G switch, this clearly improves mapped disk and transfer speed.

Q: How do I fix stuttering when playing cloud drive video?

A: Prefer players that support direct playback without transcoding, such as Jellyfin or Video Station. Also make sure the playback resolution and bitrate stay within what the drive can transfer in real time, lowering the bitrate if needed or choosing a drive without speed limits.
